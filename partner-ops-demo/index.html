What the automation does — and never does

The guardrails are rules, not habits: the same checks run for every partner, in every language.

It does

  • ✓Keeps one row per partner: country, type, official contact route
  • ✓Stores the source link that proves the route exists
  • ✓Drafts in the partner's working language (KO · EN · ZH)
  • ✓Writes the send, the reply and the rate card back on the row

It never

  • ✕Guesses an address, an employee name or a channel handle
  • ✕Uses third-party contact data
  • ✕Sends anything without a named owner's approval
  • ✕Counts an auto-reply as interest

The same rule book runs on the advertiser side as eight numbered stage rules.

Approval gate — one named owner before any send

What the owner checks

  • An official contact route on the row, never a guessed address
  • The source link, attached as evidence
  • Recipient, language and subject read once
  • Approval recorded under the owner's name

On this pipeline the send happens in the mail tool, so the gate is a state on the row. This view shows what is waiting — it is not a send button.

Why this exists

Two workflows used to live in my head and in a chat thread: who I had contacted, who replied, what they said about money, and what I owed them next. I built them into one operating layer so the team reads status instead of asking for it — and so nothing goes out without a person having approved it. The rule book doubles as the onboarding doc: a teammate can pick up either board without a handover call.

The problem
  • Status asked for in every meeting
  • Partner research in one file, calls in another
  • Outreach quality depended on who was sending
What I built
  • One row per partner and per advertiser, with the evidence attached
  • Deterministic stage rules, so the board cannot disagree with itself
  • AI drafting for the repetitive part; a human owns the send
Design choices
  • Regions reported separately — a blended total hides the bottleneck
  • Missing data reads "not confirmed", never 0
  • Auto-replies logged, excluded from reply rate

Everything on this page runs on illustrative sample data. Names, contacts, rate cards, replies and meeting notes are invented; links point to example.com and no message is sent.
